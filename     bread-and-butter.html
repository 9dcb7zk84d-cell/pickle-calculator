From harvest to jar

  1. Slice. Wash pickling cucumbers, ideally 100–125 mm long. Remove about 1.6 mm from the blossom end, then slice approximately 4.8 mm thick. Thinly slice the onions.
  2. Salt and chill. Mix cucumbers and onions with the measured salt. Cover with about 50 mm of ice. Refrigerate for 3–4 hours, adding ice as needed, then drain thoroughly.
  3. Make the syrup. Combine vinegar, sugar, mustard seed, celery seed and turmeric. Boil for 10 minutes. If using calcium chloride, measure the finished syrup, calculate the dose above, then dissolve it in the syrup.
  4. Hot pack. Add drained vegetables and slowly return to boiling. Fill suitable hot preserving jars with vegetables and syrup, leaving 13 mm headspace. Remove bubbles, check headspace, clean rims and fit lids according to their instructions.
  5. Process. Choose one of the methods below. Do not scale processing time with batch size.
  6. Cool and store. After a boiling-water process, turn off the heat, remove the lid and wait 5 minutes before lifting jars out. Cool upright, undisturbed, for 12–24 hours. Check seals; refrigerate unsealed jars. Label and store sealed jars in a cool, dark place. Allow 4–5 weeks for flavour to develop. Refrigerate after opening.

Choose your heat process

Boiling-water canning

For hot-packed jars up to 946 mL. Cover jars with at least 25 mm of water. Start timing once a full boil returns; maintain the boil for the entire process.

Or: controlled low-temperature pasteurisation

82.3–85°C for a full 30 minutes for the source’s 473 mL or 946 mL jars. Begin with canner water at 49–60°C, then add hot water to 25 mm above jars. Bring to the target range before timing. Use an accurate thermometer and keep the water in range throughout; restart the 30 minutes if it drops below 82.3°C. Higher temperatures soften the pickles.
